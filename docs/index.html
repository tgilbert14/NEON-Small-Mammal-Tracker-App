What you can do

Pick a site on the map

The landing is a national map of every NEON site — dot size is how many animals were caught, color is the dominant mammal family. Tap a dot to dive in.

Explore by species

Flip to “by species” and the map redraws to show where one animal turns up across the country — a live range map for any of 140+ species.

Read the community

Sex & age structure, a Hill-number diversity profile, body-size distributions, and breeding phenology — the whole community for a site & window.

Detection-corrected abundance

Closed-capture estimates (Schnabel / Chapman) that count the animals the traps missed, shown alongside Minimum-Number-Known-Alive with honest caveats.

Follow one animal

Every ear-tag becomes a life story — a trap-grid home-range heatmap and replay, a capture history, and a shareable holographic trading card.

Compare & report

Put two sites head-to-head, take a 30-second guided tour, or print a one-page site report card to PDF.

Size Lab — the QC workbench

Every individual on one interactive body-size map. Pick a species, tap a dot to pin its profile, and open its QC history card — every-capture measurements plus automatic, ranked data-quality flags. Download the map, the card (PNG), or the raw metadata (CSV). A QC map, not a body-condition index.

Statistics

Every view leads with transparent, well-sourced methods:

  • Abundance — Minimum Number Known Alive (Krebs 1966) and catch-per-effort, with closed-capture detection-corrected estimates (Schnabel / Chapman) shown only when within-bout recaptures (≥ 3) make them stable, and never below the number handled.
  • Diversity — Hill numbers over distinct individuals, so a frequently-recaptured animal is not double-counted; richness from species accumulation with a bias-corrected Chao1 estimate and its confidence interval.
  • Body condition — an adult weight percentile within species, rather than a scaled-mass index that would largely reflect measurement noise in these taxa.
  • Individual identity — NEON keeps a tag on one animal for life and doesn't reuse numbers, so multi-year careers are treated as the real long-lived animals they are; only the rare impossible history (e.g. one tag at two plots on a single day) is flagged. Rarity tiers reflect trappability and residency rather than ecological rarity.
